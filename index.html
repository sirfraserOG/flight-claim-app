🔎 Check the airline's reason

Reason recorded:

The legal point: the airline doesn't have to pay compensation if the disruption was caused by extraordinary circumstances that could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken. The burden of proving this is on the airline: it must show both the circumstance and that it took all reasonable measures. You don't have to prove the reason was wrong, but independent records can help you decide whether to challenge it and what evidence to ask for.

    How to document each item

    • Departure boards/app screens: photo or screenshot showing flight number, status and the clock/time.
    • Reason given: write exact words, who said it, time. Ask for it in writing (SMS/email counts).
    • Re-booking offers: note flight, times, and whether you accepted. Screenshot app/email offers.
    • Receipts: keep itemised originals (bank statements are often rejected). Photograph each one now. Spend reasonably — no alcohol, luxury hotels.
    • Arrival time: note when the aircraft door opened at your destination — that's the time that counts.
    • Boarding pass & booking ref: screenshot both; keep the confirmation email.

    5. Compensation check

    General information only — not legal advice. Outcomes depend on the facts and the airline's evidence.

    Extraordinary circumstances

    Usually NOT payable (extraordinary): severe weather, air traffic control restrictions/strikes, security risks, political instability, bird strikes, airport/third-party strikes, hidden manufacturing defects.

    Usually payable: routine technical faults, crew shortages/sickness, the airline's own staff strikes, knock-on scheduling problems within the airline's control.

    Even then, care (meals, hotel) and refund/re-routing rights still apply.

    Time limits

    • England & Wales (and N. Ireland): 6 years
    • Scotland: 5 years
    • EU: set by national law — e.g. Ireland 6 yrs, Germany 3 yrs, Spain 5 yrs, France 5 yrs, Netherlands 2 yrs (check locally)
